// THE PROBLEM

A crisis unfolding
in silence above us.

Over 11,000 active satellites operate daily under the threat of catastrophic collision — in an environment growing more crowded with every launch. The consequences extend far beyond a single lost satellite.

01
40,000+

Tracked objects in orbit

Space surveillance networks now track over 40,000 objects larger than 10 cm — of which only ~11,000 are active payloads. The rest is debris capable of ending a mission. (ESA Space Environment Report, 2025)

02
1.2 million

Untracked lethal fragments

Beyond the catalogue, ESA models estimate over 1.2 million fragments between 1–10 cm — too small to track, large enough to destroy a satellite at orbital velocity. Every conjunction carries this hidden tail.

03
Kessler

The cascade scenario

At ~550 km, debris density already rivals the number of active satellites. A single major collision can trigger a chain reaction — rendering entire orbital shells unusable for generations.

// THE SOLUTION

Decision support
for orbital safety.

CAS ingests real Space-Track conjunction data, prioritizes risks using physics-based models, and evaluates maneuver options — then tells you exactly what to do in operator language.

CDM

Real-time Space-Track integration

Directly fetches Conjunction Data Messages from US Space Command, three times a day on the published quota. No manual uploads.

Pc

Foster-Chan collision probability

Physics-based 2D Gaussian integral with Bessel I₀ — Foster's method. The numerical implementation matches the analytical closed-form reference to machine precision (cross-checked against the Marcum-Q solution), verified across 306 automated tests. Not a black box.

🎯

Decision Engine

Every conjunction gets a clear output: Maneuver advised, Monitor, or No action — with priority, confidence, and decision window.

⊞

Maneuver trade-space NEW

A grid of lead-time × ΔV options — each cell showing resulting miss distance, post-maneuver Pc, and cascade safety. Inspired by NASA CARA's trade-space approach: the operator picks the trade-off, the engine does the math.

🔗

Cascade analysis

Screens the full public catalogue against the post-maneuver trajectory — so a fix for one conjunction doesn't quietly create another. Minimum-fuel options surfaced first.

⏱

72h trend analysis + forecast

Tracks Pc evolution over 72 hours. Detects escalating vs de-escalating risk. Forecasts future direction with confidence rating.

📉

Alert reduction funnel

Hundreds of raw conjunction alerts collapse into the few that need a decision — and the handful that are truly critical. Risk-based triage cuts through alert fatigue.

🛰

EU SST integration NEW

Re-entry tracking, fragmentation events, and orbital breakup monitoring — drawn directly from Europe's own EU SST feeds, alongside Space-Track and Celestrak.

🚀

Launch schedule NEW

Real-time upcoming and recent launch tracking. Countdown timers, mission details, and launch provider information.

🏢

Operator directory NEW

Thousands of space organizations from ESA DISCOS. Searchable by category, country, and constellation. Contact details included.

Screen a planned deployment orbit against the catalog before you commit, and sweep RAAN to surface a cleaner deployment phase. Includes mission-design orbit comparison (catalog density, debris fraction, conjunction history). Decision support, not ascent COLA — a first-order launch-window proxy.

What CAS is — and what it isn't.

CAS IS
A decision-support tool that helps operators prioritize conjunction risks and evaluate safer maneuver options under increasing alert volume.
CAS IS NOT
An autonomous collision avoidance system. CAS does not execute maneuvers, does not replace SSA systems, and relies on publicly available conjunction data sources.

All maneuver decisions remain with the operator. CAS provides analysis — humans make the call.
